How to sign up, deposit and cash out a big win

Large withdrawals are where limits and source-of-funds checks bite. Set up the account so a big win can be paid.

Step 1: open your account

  1. Check the site before you sign up

    Find the licence number in the site's footer and look it up on the regulator's public register, then read our review for the owner, limits and complaint record. If the footer has no licence number, stop there.

  2. Register with your real details

    Use your legal name, date of birth, home address and an email you check, exactly as they appear on your ID. You must be 18 or over. Wrong or made-up details are the most common reason a withdrawal is refused later.

  3. Choose your currency carefully

    If NZD is offered, pick it at sign-up. Most sites will not let you change the account currency afterwards, and a non-NZD account means your bank converts every deposit and withdrawal.

  4. Set your limits first

    Before you deposit, open the account or responsible gambling settings and set a deposit limit, a loss limit and a session reminder. Lowering a limit takes effect straight away; raising one usually has a cooling-off delay.

  5. Verify your identity straight away

    Upload photo ID (passport or driver licence) and proof of address dated in the last three months, such as a bank statement or power bill. Doing it now means your first withdrawal is not held up waiting for checks.

Step 2: make a deposit

  1. Open the cashier

    Log in and choose Deposit or Cashier. Check the minimum deposit, usually around NZ$20 to NZ$30 or the euro equivalent, and whether the method you want carries a fee.

  2. Pick a method you can also withdraw to

    Cards, e-wallets such as Skrill, bank transfer and crypto all work both ways. Prepaid vouchers such as Neosurf are deposit-only, so you would need a second method to get money out.

  3. Decide on the bonus before you pay

    Tick or untick the bonus box, or leave the code field empty if you do not want it. Once a bonus is active, its wagering, maximum-bet and game rules apply to your balance until you clear or forfeit it.

  4. Enter the amount and confirm

    Stay inside the limit you set. Your bank may ask you to approve a card payment in its app. If a New Zealand bank declines the payment, it may block offshore gambling transactions; do not try to get around a block you set yourself.

  5. Check the balance and keep a record

    Card and e-wallet deposits usually show within a minute. Screenshot or note the transaction ID, the amount and the time, in case you need to raise a dispute.

Step 3: make a withdrawal

  1. Clear or cancel any bonus first

    Withdrawing while a bonus is active usually voids the bonus and anything won with it. Check your wagering progress in the account area, or forfeit the bonus if you would rather cash out what is yours.

  2. Request the withdrawal

    Open the cashier, choose Withdraw and enter the amount. Most sites send money back by the method you deposited with, so expect to use the same card, e-wallet or wallet address.

  3. Leave it pending

    Many sites hold withdrawals for 24 to 72 hours before processing them. Do not reverse the request to keep playing; turn off 'reverse withdrawal' if the site offers it.

  4. Answer verification requests quickly

    For larger amounts the site may ask for a recent bank statement or proof of where the money came from. Reply from the email on your account and keep copies of everything you send.

  5. Watch the limits

    Daily, weekly and monthly withdrawal limits mean a big win can be paid in instalments. The limit for each site is in its row above and in its review.

  6. Track the payment to your account

    Once approved, e-wallet and crypto withdrawals usually arrive the same day; cards and bank transfers take one to five working days. If nothing arrives, contact support with the transaction ID, then the regulator named on the licence.

What "best paying casino" actually means

Casinos do not set one payout rate for the whole site. Every game has its own return to player, so a site's 'payout percentage' is just an average of whatever its customers happened to play. Two things genuinely make one casino pay better than another: the RTP versions it chooses to run, and the limits it puts on how much you can withdraw. A casino running high-RTP versions but capping withdrawals at NZ$1,000 a day is not a high payout casino for anyone who wins big.

The house edge on every main game

Expected cost is house edge times the amount you wager. NZ$1,000 wagered on European roulette costs about NZ$27 on average; the same NZ$1,000 on a 94% pokie costs about NZ$60, and on basic-strategy blackjack about NZ$5.

Why RTP settings matter more than the casino

Studios commonly offer the same pokie at several RTP levels. The casino picks one. You can check which is running by opening the game's paytable or information screen and comparing the RTP with the figure on the studio's website. If a casino consistently runs the lower settings, its pokies are a worse deal everywhere in the lobby, however generous its bonus looks.

Limits: the other half of paying out

The highest published limits on our list belong to the NZD-balance sites in the Hollycorn and Novatrix groups: NZ$3,000 a day, NZ$7,500 a week and NZ$30,000 a month. At the low end, Smash and Rivo publish €1,000 a day, Smash €2,000 a week and Lucky Circus €2,500 a week. Lucky Circus, Bet&Play, Rivo and Smash also have daily win caps.

How to get the best return

  • Choose games with a low house edge: blackjack with basic strategy, baccarat banker, European roulette.
  • On pokies, check the RTP setting and prefer 96% or more.
  • Skip side bets and tie bets, which carry far higher edges.
  • Avoid bonuses whose wagering would force you to bet many times your deposit.
  • Pick a casino whose withdrawal limits match the size of win you could realistically land.

How RTP is calculated and audited

A game's RTP comes from its maths model: every possible outcome multiplied by its probability and payout. Independent labs such as GLI, iTech Labs and BMM test the random number generator and confirm the published RTP over millions of simulated rounds. The audit covers the game, not the casino, which is why the casino's chosen RTP setting matters.

Highest RTP pokies

Which blackjack rules raise or lower the payout?

Video poker

Full-pay Jacks or Better (9/6) returns about 99.5% with perfect strategy, and some Deuces Wild versions exceed 100%. The catch is the paytable: a 8/5 Jacks or Better drops to about 97.3%. Check the payout for a full house and a flush before you play.

Which live casino games pay out the most?

Live blackjack with basic strategy (about 99.5%) and baccarat on the banker bet (98.94%) lead. Live game shows such as Crazy Time return roughly 94% to 96% depending on the bet, well below the tables.

Which casino bonuses actually improve your payout?

Only low-wager ones. A bonus at 10x, like Smash's, can add value on average; at 40x, like the reported Lucky Circus offer, the expected cost of clearing it usually exceeds the bonus. Many casinos also count high-RTP games at a reduced rate or exclude them from wagering, so check the game weighting list.

Can high rollers cash out more?

Sometimes. VIP levels at the Hollycorn and Novatrix brands may lift the standard NZ$7,500 weekly limit, usually only by agreement. If you play at high stakes, ask for the VIP limit in writing before you deposit, and note that progressive jackpot wins are usually exempt from limits while normal wins are not.

Frequently asked questions

Which online casino has the highest payout in NZ?

No casino has a single payout rate. What you can compare is the RTP of the games and the withdrawal limits. The best-documented NZD sites publish the highest limits on our list, at NZ$3,000 a day and NZ$7,500 a week.

What casino game has the best odds?

Blackjack played with basic strategy, at about 0.5% house edge, followed by baccarat on the banker bet (1.06%) and European roulette (2.7%).

Is a 98% RTP pokie better than a 96% one?

Over the long run, yes: it costs about 2 cents per dollar wagered instead of 4. In a single session, volatility matters far more than that difference.

Why do some casinos have different RTP for the same game?

Studios sell many games at several RTP settings and the casino chooses which one to run. The game looks the same; only the paytable information shows the difference.

Do withdrawal limits affect payouts?

Yes. A big win is paid in instalments up to the limit, so a low weekly limit can turn a large win into months of payments. A daily win cap means winnings above it are not paid at all.

Who sets the RTP on casino games?

The game studio sets the available RTP versions; the casino chooses which one to run.

Is the RTP lower when playing with bonus funds?

No, the game's RTP is the same, but wagering requirements raise the total you must bet and so the expected cost.
