How to register, claim free spins and withdraw winnings

Even with no deposit, you will need a verified account and usually a deposit method before free spin winnings can be withdrawn.

Step 1: open your account

  1. Check the site before you sign up

    Find the licence number in the site's footer and look it up on the regulator's public register, then read our review for the owner, limits and complaint record. If the footer has no licence number, stop there.

  2. Register with your real details

    Use your legal name, date of birth, home address and an email you check, exactly as they appear on your ID. You must be 18 or over. Wrong or made-up details are the most common reason a withdrawal is refused later.

  3. Choose your currency carefully

    If NZD is offered, pick it at sign-up. Most sites will not let you change the account currency afterwards, and a non-NZD account means your bank converts every deposit and withdrawal.

  4. Set your limits first

    Before you deposit, open the account or responsible gambling settings and set a deposit limit, a loss limit and a session reminder. Lowering a limit takes effect straight away; raising one usually has a cooling-off delay.

  5. Verify your identity straight away

    Upload photo ID (passport or driver licence) and proof of address dated in the last three months, such as a bank statement or power bill. Doing it now means your first withdrawal is not held up waiting for checks.

Step 2: make a deposit

  1. Open the cashier

    Log in and choose Deposit or Cashier. Check the minimum deposit, usually around NZ$20 to NZ$30 or the euro equivalent, and whether the method you want carries a fee.

  2. Pick a method you can also withdraw to

    Cards, e-wallets such as Skrill, bank transfer and crypto all work both ways. Prepaid vouchers such as Neosurf are deposit-only, so you would need a second method to get money out.

  3. Decide on the bonus before you pay

    Tick or untick the bonus box, or leave the code field empty if you do not want it. Once a bonus is active, its wagering, maximum-bet and game rules apply to your balance until you clear or forfeit it.

  4. Enter the amount and confirm

    Stay inside the limit you set. Your bank may ask you to approve a card payment in its app. If a New Zealand bank declines the payment, it may block offshore gambling transactions; do not try to get around a block you set yourself.

  5. Check the balance and keep a record

    Card and e-wallet deposits usually show within a minute. Screenshot or note the transaction ID, the amount and the time, in case you need to raise a dispute.

Step 3: make a withdrawal

  1. Clear or cancel any bonus first

    Withdrawing while a bonus is active usually voids the bonus and anything won with it. Check your wagering progress in the account area, or forfeit the bonus if you would rather cash out what is yours.

  2. Request the withdrawal

    Open the cashier, choose Withdraw and enter the amount. Most sites send money back by the method you deposited with, so expect to use the same card, e-wallet or wallet address.

  3. Leave it pending

    Many sites hold withdrawals for 24 to 72 hours before processing them. Do not reverse the request to keep playing; turn off 'reverse withdrawal' if the site offers it.

  4. Answer verification requests quickly

    For larger amounts the site may ask for a recent bank statement or proof of where the money came from. Reply from the email on your account and keep copies of everything you send.

  5. Watch the limits

    Daily, weekly and monthly withdrawal limits mean a big win can be paid in instalments. The limit for each site is in its row above and in its review.

  6. Track the payment to your account

    Once approved, e-wallet and crypto withdrawals usually arrive the same day; cards and bank transfers take one to five working days. If nothing arrives, contact support with the transaction ID, then the regulator named on the licence.

What a no deposit bonus is

A no deposit bonus gives a new player a small amount of play without paying in first: free spins on a named pokie, a few dollars of bonus cash, or occasionally free play for a short time. Casinos use them to get you to register and verify an account. They are genuinely free to claim, but the terms are designed so that very few players turn them into withdrawable money.

Free spins vs bonus cash

The catch: win caps and wagering

Almost every no deposit offer carries a maximum cashout, commonly NZ$50 to NZ$100, regardless of what you win. Winnings also usually need wagering, often at a higher multiple than deposit bonuses, and many casinos require a small deposit before you can withdraw anything at all, to verify your payment method.

A worked example

Take 20 free spins at NZ$0.20 on a 96% RTP pokie. The spins are worth NZ$4 of stake, and on average return about NZ$3.84. Suppose you win NZ$10. With 40x wagering on winnings, you must bet NZ$400; on a 96% game that costs about NZ$16 on average, more than the NZ$10 you won. Most players finish with nothing, a few clear the wagering, and the max cashout caps the lucky ones. That is the honest value of most no deposit offers: some entertainment and a look at the casino.

Spotting fake no deposit offers

  • Huge no deposit amounts (hundreds of dollars) with no visible terms.
  • Requests to pay a 'verification fee' or 'tax' to release winnings.
  • Offers sent by text message or social media from accounts that are not the casino's.
  • Sites with no licence number, or a number that is not on the regulator's register.

Never pay to claim

No legitimate casino asks you to pay to receive a bonus or to unlock a withdrawal.

Claiming one sensibly

  1. Read the full terms first

    Find the wagering, max cashout, eligible games and time limit.

  2. Check the casino, not the offer

    A free offer at a casino with no verified licence is not worth your personal details.

  3. Verify early

    You will need ID to withdraw anyway.

  4. Treat it as a trial

    Use it to judge the site's support and cashier, not as a way to make money.

How to claim a no deposit bonus in five steps

  1. Choose a casino you have checked

    A free offer at an unlicensed site is still a risk to your details.

  2. Create your account with accurate details

    Name, date of birth and address must match your ID.

  3. Verify your email or mobile number

    Most casinos only credit the bonus after the confirmation link or code.

  4. Enter the bonus code if one is required

    In the cashier or promotions page, before you play.

  5. Open the eligible game

    Free spins only work on the named pokie at a fixed stake.

Turning a no deposit bonus into real money: four rules

  1. Complete the wagering

    Usually 40x to 60x the winnings from free spins.

  2. Stay within the max bet

    Often NZ$5 or less per spin; one bigger bet can void everything.

  3. Respect the max cashout

    Typically NZ$50 to NZ$100, whatever you win.

  4. Verify before withdrawing

    And expect to make a small deposit first at many casinos, to confirm a payment method.

Types of no deposit bonus

Best games to use a no deposit bonus on

You rarely get to choose: free spins are tied to one pokie. For bonus credit, low-volatility pokies such as Starburst or Blood Suckers stretch a small balance through wagering further than high-volatility games.

No deposit offers for existing players

Loyalty programmes, birthday spins and cashback are the realistic 'no deposit' offers once you have an account. Cashback with no wagering is the cleanest, because what you receive is real money.

Key terms: expiry, excluded games and withdrawal amounts

No deposit bonuses often expire within 24 hours to 7 days if unused, and winnings may need wagering within a few days more. Excluded games and the max cashout sit in the bonus terms, not the banner.

Frequently asked questions

Are there real no deposit bonuses in NZ?

Offshore casinos do run them from time to time, though we have none confirmed on record right now. They are real but small, and they almost always cap what you can win.

Can you win real money with a no deposit bonus?

Yes, but only up to the maximum cashout, commonly NZ$50 to NZ$100, and only after completing wagering. Many casinos also require a deposit before your first withdrawal.

What is a max cashout?

The most you can withdraw from bonus winnings, however much you win. Anything above it is removed when you withdraw.

Do no deposit free spins have wagering?

Usually yes: winnings from the spins are credited as bonus money and must be wagered. Wager-free spins exist but are rare.

Why do casinos ask for ID for a free bonus?

Licensed offshore casinos must verify customers before paying out, and verification also stops people claiming the same offer many times.

Can I get multiple no deposit bonuses from the same casino?

One per person, household and payment method is the usual rule. Sister sites may also count as the same casino.

Are no deposit bonuses really free?

They cost no money to claim, but wagering, caps and the usual need to deposit before withdrawing mean the value is small.
