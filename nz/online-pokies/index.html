How to start playing pokies for real money, step by step

Open an account, fund it and cash out in the right order, so your first pokies win is not stuck behind verification.

Step 1: open your account

  1. Check the site before you sign up

    Find the licence number in the site's footer and look it up on the regulator's public register, then read our review for the owner, limits and complaint record. If the footer has no licence number, stop there.

  2. Register with your real details

    Use your legal name, date of birth, home address and an email you check, exactly as they appear on your ID. You must be 18 or over. Wrong or made-up details are the most common reason a withdrawal is refused later.

  3. Choose your currency carefully

    If NZD is offered, pick it at sign-up. Most sites will not let you change the account currency afterwards, and a non-NZD account means your bank converts every deposit and withdrawal.

  4. Set your limits first

    Before you deposit, open the account or responsible gambling settings and set a deposit limit, a loss limit and a session reminder. Lowering a limit takes effect straight away; raising one usually has a cooling-off delay.

  5. Verify your identity straight away

    Upload photo ID (passport or driver licence) and proof of address dated in the last three months, such as a bank statement or power bill. Doing it now means your first withdrawal is not held up waiting for checks.

Step 2: make a deposit

  1. Open the cashier

    Log in and choose Deposit or Cashier. Check the minimum deposit, usually around NZ$20 to NZ$30 or the euro equivalent, and whether the method you want carries a fee.

  2. Pick a method you can also withdraw to

    Cards, e-wallets such as Skrill, bank transfer and crypto all work both ways. Prepaid vouchers such as Neosurf are deposit-only, so you would need a second method to get money out.

  3. Decide on the bonus before you pay

    Tick or untick the bonus box, or leave the code field empty if you do not want it. Once a bonus is active, its wagering, maximum-bet and game rules apply to your balance until you clear or forfeit it.

  4. Enter the amount and confirm

    Stay inside the limit you set. Your bank may ask you to approve a card payment in its app. If a New Zealand bank declines the payment, it may block offshore gambling transactions; do not try to get around a block you set yourself.

  5. Check the balance and keep a record

    Card and e-wallet deposits usually show within a minute. Screenshot or note the transaction ID, the amount and the time, in case you need to raise a dispute.

Step 3: make a withdrawal

  1. Clear or cancel any bonus first

    Withdrawing while a bonus is active usually voids the bonus and anything won with it. Check your wagering progress in the account area, or forfeit the bonus if you would rather cash out what is yours.

  2. Request the withdrawal

    Open the cashier, choose Withdraw and enter the amount. Most sites send money back by the method you deposited with, so expect to use the same card, e-wallet or wallet address.

  3. Leave it pending

    Many sites hold withdrawals for 24 to 72 hours before processing them. Do not reverse the request to keep playing; turn off 'reverse withdrawal' if the site offers it.

  4. Answer verification requests quickly

    For larger amounts the site may ask for a recent bank statement or proof of where the money came from. Reply from the email on your account and keep copies of everything you send.

  5. Watch the limits

    Daily, weekly and monthly withdrawal limits mean a big win can be paid in instalments. The limit for each site is in its row above and in its review.

  6. Track the payment to your account

    Once approved, e-wallet and crypto withdrawals usually arrive the same day; cards and bank transfers take one to five working days. If nothing arrives, contact support with the transaction ID, then the regulator named on the licence.

How online pokies work

Every online pokie is run by a random number generator (RNG): a program that picks a result for each spin the moment you press the button. The reels you see are an animation of a result that has already been decided. Spins are independent, so a machine is never 'due' a win and a run of losses does not make the next spin any more likely to pay. Games from the major studios are tested by independent labs that check the RNG and the published maths.

What you can control is which game you play, at what stake, for how long, and at which RTP setting. Those choices change your expected cost far more than any strategy on the reels.

RTP, and why the same pokie can pay differently

Return to player (RTP) is the share of all money staked on a game that it pays back over a very large number of spins. A 96% RTP pokie returns NZ$96 for every NZ$100 staked on average, so the expected cost is about 4 cents per dollar wagered. Over one session, results swing wildly; RTP only describes the long run.

The detail most guides leave out: many popular pokies are sold to casinos at several RTP settings, for example 96.5%, 94% and 91%. The game looks identical. The casino chooses the version. On NZ$2,000 of spins, the difference between 96% and 91% is about NZ$100 in expected losses.

How to check the RTP a casino is running

Open the game's information or paytable screen (often an 'i' or menu icon) and look for 'RTP' or 'theoretical return'. If the figure shown is lower than the one on the studio's own website, the casino is running a lower setting.

Volatility: how a pokie spends your money

Volatility (or variance) describes how results are spread. Low-volatility pokies pay small amounts often, so a budget lasts longer. High-volatility pokies pay rarely but can pay big, so long losing runs are normal. Two games with the same 96% RTP can feel completely different: one drip-feeds wins, the other can take your whole session budget before a bonus round lands. Choose volatility to match your budget, not your hopes.

The main types of online pokie

Progressive jackpots

A progressive jackpot grows with every bet placed on the game across all the casinos linked to it, until one player wins it. The headline can run into millions, but the chance of hitting it on any spin is tiny and the base game usually has a lower RTP to pay for the pot. If you play jackpot pokies, check the casino's withdrawal limits first: several sites on this list cap withdrawals at a few thousand dollars a week, and some exempt progressive jackpots from those limits while others do not.

Free demo play

Most studios offer a free demo mode, either on the casino site or on the studio's own website. Demo play is the best way to learn a game's volatility and features without spending anything. Remember that some demo versions run at the highest RTP setting, which may be higher than the real-money version a casino offers.

Pokie symbols and how to read a paytable

Every pokie has an information screen, usually behind an 'i' or menu icon. It is the only reliable place to find the game's RTP and rules.

  1. Open the paytable before you spin

    Look for the i, ? or menu icon in the game. It works the same in demo and real-money mode.

  2. Find the RTP figure

    Usually in the rules text. If it shows several values, the casino chose one; if none is shown, assume the lowest.

  3. Learn the special symbols

    Wilds substitute for other symbols, scatters trigger features regardless of position, and bonus symbols start free spins or pick games.

  4. Check paylines or ways to win

    Fixed paylines pay left to right on set lines; 'ways' games pay matching symbols on adjacent reels, up to 117,649 ways on Megaways.

  5. Note the max win

    Shown as a multiple of your stake, such as 5,000x. Once reached, the round ends even if more wins were due.

What do max win caps mean on a pokie?

A max win cap is the most a single round can pay, set as a multiple of your stake. A 5,000x cap at NZ$1 a spin means NZ$5,000. Casinos can add their own caps on top: Lucky Circus and Bet&Play do not pay net daily winnings above US$50,000, and Smash and Rivo cap daily wins at €100,000.

In-game free spins vs bonus spins

In-game free spins are a feature of the pokie, triggered by scatters and paid as normal winnings. Bonus spins come from the casino's promotion, are tied to one game and a fixed stake, and their winnings usually carry wagering. Lucky Circus's reported 450 free spins are bonus spins at 40x; a scatter-triggered round in Book of Dead is not.

Pokies tournaments

Tournaments rank players by points, biggest multiplier or total wins over a set period, with a prize pool shared among the leaders. Entry is usually free with real-money spins on the listed games. Read the scoring rule: 'biggest multiplier' tournaments reward small stakes, while 'total wagered' ones reward volume and can push you to spend more than you planned.

Frequently asked questions

Which online pokies pay out the most in NZ?

Look for games with an RTP of 96% or more, and check the setting in the game's information panel, because the same title can run at a lower RTP at some casinos. No pokie 'pays more' in the short term; RTP is a long-run average.

Can you win real money on online pokies?

Yes, but on average every pokie returns less than you stake. Wins are random, and over time the house edge means most players lose more than they win. Play with money you are happy to spend on entertainment.

Are online pokies rigged?

Games from the major studios are tested by independent labs and use certified random number generators. The real risk is less the game than the casino: which RTP version it runs and whether it pays out. That is what our reviews check.

What is the difference between RTP and volatility?

RTP tells you how much a pokie pays back on average over the long run. Volatility tells you how unevenly it pays: often and small, or rarely and big.

Do I pay tax on pokie winnings in New Zealand?

Not if you play for fun. Casual gambling winnings are not treated as income by Inland Revenue. See gambling winnings tax.

Are free pokies the same as real-money pokies?

The game and features are the same, but the demo may run at a different RTP setting from the real-money version a casino offers, and demo wins cannot be withdrawn.

What is the difference between pokies and slots?

None. 'Pokies' is the Australian and New Zealand name for slot machines, from 'poker machine'.

Do pokies pay out more at certain times of day?

No. Each spin is decided by a random number generator, independent of the time and of previous spins.

What changes for NZ pokies players on 1 December 2026?
