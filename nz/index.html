How to open an account, deposit and withdraw at an online casino

The same steps apply at every casino on this list. Doing them in this order avoids the problems readers report most often: refused withdrawals and long verification holds.

Step 1: open your account

  1. Check the site before you sign up

    Find the licence number in the site's footer and look it up on the regulator's public register, then read our review for the owner, limits and complaint record. If the footer has no licence number, stop there.

  2. Register with your real details

    Use your legal name, date of birth, home address and an email you check, exactly as they appear on your ID. You must be 18 or over. Wrong or made-up details are the most common reason a withdrawal is refused later.

  3. Choose your currency carefully

    If NZD is offered, pick it at sign-up. Most sites will not let you change the account currency afterwards, and a non-NZD account means your bank converts every deposit and withdrawal.

  4. Set your limits first

    Before you deposit, open the account or responsible gambling settings and set a deposit limit, a loss limit and a session reminder. Lowering a limit takes effect straight away; raising one usually has a cooling-off delay.

  5. Verify your identity straight away

    Upload photo ID (passport or driver licence) and proof of address dated in the last three months, such as a bank statement or power bill. Doing it now means your first withdrawal is not held up waiting for checks.

Step 2: make a deposit

  1. Open the cashier

    Log in and choose Deposit or Cashier. Check the minimum deposit, usually around NZ$20 to NZ$30 or the euro equivalent, and whether the method you want carries a fee.

  2. Pick a method you can also withdraw to

    Cards, e-wallets such as Skrill, bank transfer and crypto all work both ways. Prepaid vouchers such as Neosurf are deposit-only, so you would need a second method to get money out.

  3. Decide on the bonus before you pay

    Tick or untick the bonus box, or leave the code field empty if you do not want it. Once a bonus is active, its wagering, maximum-bet and game rules apply to your balance until you clear or forfeit it.

  4. Enter the amount and confirm

    Stay inside the limit you set. Your bank may ask you to approve a card payment in its app. If a New Zealand bank declines the payment, it may block offshore gambling transactions; do not try to get around a block you set yourself.

  5. Check the balance and keep a record

    Card and e-wallet deposits usually show within a minute. Screenshot or note the transaction ID, the amount and the time, in case you need to raise a dispute.

Step 3: make a withdrawal

  1. Clear or cancel any bonus first

    Withdrawing while a bonus is active usually voids the bonus and anything won with it. Check your wagering progress in the account area, or forfeit the bonus if you would rather cash out what is yours.

  2. Request the withdrawal

    Open the cashier, choose Withdraw and enter the amount. Most sites send money back by the method you deposited with, so expect to use the same card, e-wallet or wallet address.

  3. Leave it pending

    Many sites hold withdrawals for 24 to 72 hours before processing them. Do not reverse the request to keep playing; turn off 'reverse withdrawal' if the site offers it.

  4. Answer verification requests quickly

    For larger amounts the site may ask for a recent bank statement or proof of where the money came from. Reply from the email on your account and keep copies of everything you send.

  5. Watch the limits

    Daily, weekly and monthly withdrawal limits mean a big win can be paid in instalments. The limit for each site is in its row above and in its review.

  6. Track the payment to your account

    Once approved, e-wallet and crypto withdrawals usually arrive the same day; cards and bank transfers take one to five working days. If nothing arrives, contact support with the transaction ID, then the regulator named on the licence.

Real questions New Zealanders ask about online casinos

These come from the searches New Zealanders actually type, from New Zealand Reddit threads and from Trustpilot complaints, answered in full.

Can an online casino refuse to pay out or hold my winnings?

Yes, within its terms, and that is the most common complaint we see. A casino can delay or refuse a withdrawal if verification is incomplete, if a bonus term was broken (most often the maximum bet while a bonus is active), if the withdrawal exceeds its limits, or if it suspects duplicate accounts or 'irregular play'. Some go further than their terms allow. Your protection depends on the licence: the operator's complaints process first, then the regulator named on the licence. An offshore licence offers far less than a New Zealand one will, which is why this site leads with licence and owner details. Keep screenshots of your balance, bets and every message.

Can online casinos ban you for winning or close your account?

Offshore casinos usually reserve the right to close accounts at their discretion. A legitimate casino should still pay a balance that was won within the terms. Closures after a big win are often justified by bonus abuse, multiple accounts or 'advantage play' clauses, so read those terms before you take a bonus. Bookmakers restricting consistently winning customers is a separate, long-standing practice, and New Zealand punters on Reddit report it at both offshore books and TAB NZ.

Can online casinos change the RTP or rig the games?

A casino cannot change how a certified game behaves spin by spin, but it can choose which RTP version of a game to run, and many popular pokies are sold at several settings. The difference between 96% and 91% is about NZ$5 more lost per NZ$100 wagered on average. Check the RTP in the game's information panel and compare it with the studio's published figure. One in five recent Trustpilot complaints we read claimed games were rigged; most describe ordinary losing runs, which high-volatility pokies produce often.

Why is my withdrawal taking so long, and does the weekend matter?

A withdrawal has three stages: the casino's own pending period, verification, and the payment method. Many casinos approve withdrawals manually and work reduced hours at weekends, and international transfers generally do not settle on weekends or public holidays, so a request made on Friday night can easily take until Tuesday. Verify your account straight after sign-up, withdraw by the method you deposited with, and avoid having an active bonus. See fast payout casinos.

What is a 'no verification' casino, and should I use one?

'No verification' or 'no KYC' casinos let you play and sometimes withdraw without ID checks, usually with crypto. They are the lightest-regulated end of the market. Licensed casinos must check identity under anti-money-laundering rules, and licensed New Zealand casinos will have to verify that players are 18 or over. A casino that never checks who you are is also one that can more easily refuse a large withdrawal by asking for documents at the last minute.

My bank is asking about a big win from an overseas casino. What now?

Banks must monitor large or unusual international payments under anti-money-laundering law, so a large deposit from an offshore gambling company can trigger questions. Answer them: keep the casino's transaction history, withdrawal confirmations and your ID documents, and explain the source. Winnings from casual gambling are not taxable income in New Zealand, so there is normally nothing to declare to Inland Revenue, but your bank still needs to understand where the money came from.

Do online casinos take tax off my winnings?

No casino on this list deducts New Zealand tax, and New Zealand does not tax casual gambling winnings. The '30% withholding' figure you may see online applies to non-residents winning at US-based operators. Beware of scams: anyone asking you to pay a 'tax' or 'release fee' before receiving a prize or withdrawal is trying to defraud you. See gambling winnings tax.

A low minimum deposit is good for trying a site cheaply, but check two other numbers: the minimum deposit needed to qualify for any bonus (often NZ$20 to NZ$35 or the euro equivalent), and the minimum withdrawal. Some payment methods also have their own minimums. A NZ$10 deposit that cannot be withdrawn until it grows past a NZ$50 minimum is not really a NZ$10 casino.

Lotto NZ is the lawful national lottery operator and sells lottery and instant games online through MyLotto, under New Zealand rules. What happens to my money if a casino closes or leaves New Zealand?

Offshore casinos are not required to hold player balances separately from their own money, and no New Zealand compensation scheme covers them. If a casino closes, or stops serving New Zealand on 1 December 2026 as every casino on this site is expected to, your balance is only as safe as the operator's willingness to pay it. Withdraw before any deadline, do not leave large balances sitting in an account, complete verification early so a withdrawal is not held up, and save your full transaction history.

How do I complain about an online casino?

Start with the casino's own complaints process and ask for a written reference. Red flags that should stop you depositing

  • No licence number, or a number that does not appear on the regulator's register.
  • No company name in the terms and conditions.
  • Daily win caps or weekly withdrawal limits that are low compared with the bonus on offer.
  • Bonus terms that let the casino void winnings for 'irregular play' without defining it.
  • A cluster of complaints about withdrawals being delayed after big wins, or accounts being closed.
  • Pressure: countdown timers, VIP managers who contact you to deposit, or offers to 'unlock' a withdrawal for a fee.

Never pay to withdraw

Any request to pay a fee, tax or 'unlock charge' before a withdrawal is a scam. Legitimate casinos deduct any fees from the withdrawal itself.

Gambling safely

Treat gambling as entertainment you pay for. Decide your budget before you play, set a deposit limit in your account, and stop when it is gone. Signs that it is time to step back include chasing losses, borrowing to gamble, hiding it from whānau, or feeling anxious when you are not playing.

How to make a complaint at an online casino
