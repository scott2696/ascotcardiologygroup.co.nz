How to open an account and deposit or withdraw with crypto

Crypto is fast, but mistakes with addresses and networks cannot be reversed. Follow these steps in order.

Step 1: open your account

  1. Check the site before you sign up

    Find the licence number in the site's footer and look it up on the regulator's public register, then read our review for the owner, limits and complaint record. If the footer has no licence number, stop there.

  2. Register with your real details

    Use your legal name, date of birth, home address and an email you check, exactly as they appear on your ID. You must be 18 or over. Wrong or made-up details are the most common reason a withdrawal is refused later.

  3. Choose your currency carefully

    If NZD is offered, pick it at sign-up. Most sites will not let you change the account currency afterwards, and a non-NZD account means your bank converts every deposit and withdrawal.

  4. Set your limits first

    Before you deposit, open the account or responsible gambling settings and set a deposit limit, a loss limit and a session reminder. Lowering a limit takes effect straight away; raising one usually has a cooling-off delay.

  5. Verify your identity straight away

    Upload photo ID (passport or driver licence) and proof of address dated in the last three months, such as a bank statement or power bill. Doing it now means your first withdrawal is not held up waiting for checks.

Step 2: make a deposit

  1. Buy crypto on a New Zealand exchange

    Use an exchange registered on New Zealand's Financial Service Providers Register, verify your account and buy with NZD by bank transfer. USDT avoids price swings; Litecoin and Tron-network USDT keep network fees low.

  2. Copy the casino's deposit address and network

    In the cashier, choose the coin and the network (for example USDT on TRC-20). Copy the address exactly. Sending on the wrong network usually loses the money for good.

  3. Send a small test amount first

    Send a few dollars' worth and wait for it to arrive before sending the rest. Check the casino's minimum, as amounts below it may not be credited.

  4. Wait for confirmations

    The deposit shows once the network confirms it: minutes for most coins, longer for Bitcoin when the network is busy.

  5. Record the price and the transaction hash

    Note the NZD value on the day and the transaction hash. Inland Revenue can tax gains on crypto you hold, so you need these records whether or not you win.

Step 3: make a withdrawal

  1. Clear or cancel any bonus first

    An active bonus usually has to be wagered or forfeited before crypto can be withdrawn.

  2. Paste your own wallet address and pick the same network

    Use a wallet or exchange address in your name. Double-check the network matches; a mismatch can lose the funds.

  3. Wait for approval and confirmations

    Crypto withdrawals are often approved within hours, then arrive once the network confirms them.

  4. Convert to NZD and withdraw to your bank

    Sell on your New Zealand exchange and withdraw to your bank account. Keep the records for Inland Revenue.

How crypto casinos work

A crypto casino gives you a wallet address for each coin it accepts. You send coins from your own wallet or exchange account, the casino credits your balance once the transaction is confirmed on the blockchain, and withdrawals go back to an address you supply. Most casinos here accept crypto alongside cards; Spino's cashier is built around crypto, with no NZD option. Some casinos convert your crypto into a fiat balance (euros or US dollars) for play, which adds an exchange step at both ends.

Which coins, and what they cost to move

Check the network

Sending a coin on the wrong network, for example USDT on Ethereum to an address that expects TRC-20, can lose the funds permanently. Copy the address and network exactly from the casino's cashier.

From NZ dollars to crypto and back

Most New Zealanders buy crypto on a local exchange with NZD from a bank account, then send it to the casino. To cash out, you send it back to the exchange, sell for NZD and withdraw to your bank. Each step can carry a fee: the exchange's trading fee or spread, the network fee, and any withdrawal fee. Some New Zealand banks look closely at transfers to and from exchanges, so use a registered exchange and keep records.

Risks of gambling with crypto

  • Price swings. A Bitcoin balance can lose value while you play, separate from any gambling loss. USDT avoids this.
  • Irreversible transfers. There is no chargeback if something goes wrong.
  • Less oversight. Crypto-only casinos often have the lightest licensing.
  • Faster spending. Quick deposits make it easier to spend more than you planned; set a limit first.

Crypto and Inland Revenue

Casual gambling winnings are not taxable in New Zealand, but crypto is treated as property by Inland Revenue. If you buy crypto and it rises in value before you sell it, that gain can be taxable, whether or not you gambled with it. Keep records of what you bought and sold, and when. Crypto-only vs hybrid casinos

Spino is crypto-only: no NZD, and USD is the only fiat option. The Hollycorn and Novatrix brands are hybrids: you can hold NZD and still deposit with Bitcoin or Ethereum. For most Kiwis a hybrid is simpler, because winnings can go straight back to a New Zealand bank.

What is provably fair?

Provably fair games publish a cryptographic hash of each round's result before you bet, plus a seed you can change. Afterwards you can check that the result matched the hash. It proves the round was not altered; it does not change the house edge.

Do crypto casinos require KYC verification?

Licensed ones do, usually before your first withdrawal or above a set amount. 'No KYC' marketing usually means checks are delayed, not skipped, and that is a risk when you try to withdraw a large win.

Crypto casino bonuses

Crypto offers are often quoted in coins rather than dollars. Spino's reported offer is 20% cashback up to 2,000 USDT with no wagering, which is easier to value than a large match bonus. Check whether the bonus is credited in the coin you deposited and whether wagering is counted in its dollar value.

Can a crypto casino pay out to my bank account?

Not directly from a crypto-only site. Withdraw to your own wallet, sell on a New Zealand exchange and withdraw NZD to your bank. Hybrid casinos can pay NZD by bank transfer instead.

What if I send crypto on the wrong network?

  1. Stop and do not send more

    Check the transaction hash on a block explorer to see where the funds went.

  2. Contact the casino's support

    Give the hash, coin, network and amount. Some casinos can recover funds sent on a compatible network, for a fee.

  3. Expect the worst on incompatible networks

    Funds sent on a network the casino's wallet does not support are usually lost.

  4. Prevent it next time

    Always send a small test amount first and match the network name exactly.

Frequently asked questions

Are crypto casinos legal in New Zealand?

Crypto casinos are offshore online casinos like any other. Playing is not an offence for individuals, but they are not licensed in New Zealand, cannot lawfully advertise here, and must stop serving New Zealand from 1 December 2026 unless they have applied for a licence. None of the crypto casinos on this page is run by a company that won a place in the October 2026 licence auction.

Which crypto is best for online casinos?

For low fees and no price risk, USDT on a low-fee network; for speed and low cost, Litecoin. Bitcoin is accepted most widely but can be slow and expensive when the network is busy.

Are crypto casino withdrawals faster?

Often, once a withdrawal is approved, because blockchain transfers settle in minutes. Verification still applies at most casinos on this list.

Do I pay tax on crypto casino winnings in NZ?

The gambling winnings themselves are generally not taxable, but gains on crypto you hold can be. Keep records.

Can I deposit NZD and withdraw crypto?

At many casinos, yes, but you may be asked to withdraw by your deposit method first. Check the cashier rules.

Does crypto price volatility affect my casino balance?

Yes, if the casino holds your balance in that coin. Stablecoins such as USDT avoid it.

Can I use Bitcoin anonymously at online casinos?

Not at licensed casinos, which verify identity before withdrawals. The blockchain itself is public, not anonymous.
