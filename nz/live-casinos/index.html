How to sign up and fund a live casino account

Live tables often have higher minimum bets than pokies, so set your limits before your first deposit.

Step 1: open your account

  1. Check the site before you sign up

    Find the licence number in the site's footer and look it up on the regulator's public register, then read our review for the owner, limits and complaint record. If the footer has no licence number, stop there.

  2. Register with your real details

    Use your legal name, date of birth, home address and an email you check, exactly as they appear on your ID. You must be 18 or over. Wrong or made-up details are the most common reason a withdrawal is refused later.

  3. Choose your currency carefully

    If NZD is offered, pick it at sign-up. Most sites will not let you change the account currency afterwards, and a non-NZD account means your bank converts every deposit and withdrawal.

  4. Set your limits first

    Before you deposit, open the account or responsible gambling settings and set a deposit limit, a loss limit and a session reminder. Lowering a limit takes effect straight away; raising one usually has a cooling-off delay.

  5. Verify your identity straight away

    Upload photo ID (passport or driver licence) and proof of address dated in the last three months, such as a bank statement or power bill. Doing it now means your first withdrawal is not held up waiting for checks.

Step 2: make a deposit

  1. Open the cashier

    Log in and choose Deposit or Cashier. Check the minimum deposit, usually around NZ$20 to NZ$30 or the euro equivalent, and whether the method you want carries a fee.

  2. Pick a method you can also withdraw to

    Cards, e-wallets such as Skrill, bank transfer and crypto all work both ways. Prepaid vouchers such as Neosurf are deposit-only, so you would need a second method to get money out.

  3. Decide on the bonus before you pay

    Tick or untick the bonus box, or leave the code field empty if you do not want it. Once a bonus is active, its wagering, maximum-bet and game rules apply to your balance until you clear or forfeit it.

  4. Enter the amount and confirm

    Stay inside the limit you set. Your bank may ask you to approve a card payment in its app. If a New Zealand bank declines the payment, it may block offshore gambling transactions; do not try to get around a block you set yourself.

  5. Check the balance and keep a record

    Card and e-wallet deposits usually show within a minute. Screenshot or note the transaction ID, the amount and the time, in case you need to raise a dispute.

Step 3: make a withdrawal

  1. Clear or cancel any bonus first

    Withdrawing while a bonus is active usually voids the bonus and anything won with it. Check your wagering progress in the account area, or forfeit the bonus if you would rather cash out what is yours.

  2. Request the withdrawal

    Open the cashier, choose Withdraw and enter the amount. Most sites send money back by the method you deposited with, so expect to use the same card, e-wallet or wallet address.

  3. Leave it pending

    Many sites hold withdrawals for 24 to 72 hours before processing them. Do not reverse the request to keep playing; turn off 'reverse withdrawal' if the site offers it.

  4. Answer verification requests quickly

    For larger amounts the site may ask for a recent bank statement or proof of where the money came from. Reply from the email on your account and keep copies of everything you send.

  5. Watch the limits

    Daily, weekly and monthly withdrawal limits mean a big win can be paid in instalments. The limit for each site is in its row above and in its review.

  6. Track the payment to your account

    Once approved, e-wallet and crypto withdrawals usually arrive the same day; cards and bank transfers take one to five working days. If nothing arrives, contact support with the transaction ID, then the regulator named on the licence.

How live dealer casino games work

A live casino streams a real dealer, real cards and a real wheel from a studio, usually in Europe, Asia or Latin America. You place bets on an on-screen overlay during a betting window, then watch the result. Optical character recognition reads the cards and wheel results so the game can settle your bets automatically. Most casinos on this list carry tables from the large live studios, so the same games appear in many lobbies.

Live blackjack

Live blackjack uses the same rules as computer blackjack, so basic strategy keeps the house edge around 0.5%. Check two rules before you sit down: whether blackjack pays 3:2 (good) or 6:5 (much worse), and whether the dealer hits or stands on soft 17. Many live tables have a fixed number of seats; 'infinite' versions let any number of players play the same hand. Side bets such as Perfect Pairs and 21+3 carry much higher edges.

Live roulette

Choose European (single zero, 2.7% edge) or French (single zero, with La Partage on even-money bets, about 1.35%) over American (double zero, 5.26%). Multiplier variants such as Lightning Roulette add random multipliers on straight-up numbers but pay less on standard straight-up wins to fund them.

Live baccarat

Baccarat is a game of pure chance with three bets: banker (1.06% edge after commission), player (1.24%) and tie (about 14.4%). 'No commission' tables usually pay less on certain banker wins instead. Speed baccarat deals faster rounds, which means more bets per hour and more expected cost per hour.

Live game shows

Game shows such as money wheels with bonus rounds are entertainment-first. Their RTP varies by bet, often between about 95% and 97%, and the top multipliers are rare by design. They move fast, so set a time limit as well as a money limit.

Playing at New Zealand hours

Because most studios are in Europe, the busiest tables are often New Zealand early mornings, and New Zealand evenings line up with European mornings when fewer tables may be open. Studios in Asia are closer to New Zealand time. Stake ranges are set per table, commonly from NZ$1 to NZ$5 minimum on mainstream tables, with VIP tables much higher.

Etiquette, chat and connection

  • Use a stable connection; if you drop out mid-hand, the table's rules decide what happens to your bet.
  • Chat is moderated; dealers will not respond to abuse.
  • Take screenshots of any disputed result and note the round ID shown on screen.

Live poker and live craps

Live casino poker, such as Casino Hold'em and Three Card Poker, is played against the dealer, not other players, with house edges of about 2% to 3.5% on the main bet. Live craps follows standard rules; the pass line edge is 1.41% and odds bets carry none.

Live casino bonuses: do they work on live games?

Mostly not. Welcome bonuses usually weight live games at 0% to 10% towards wagering, so a 40x bonus would take ten times longer to clear. Look for live cashback instead, or play live games with your own money and leave bonuses for pokies.

How much do you need for a live session?

A simple rule from blackjack players: bring 20 to 40 times the table minimum. At a NZ$5 table that is NZ$100 to NZ$200. At 60 hands an hour and a 0.5% edge with basic strategy, the average cost is about NZ$1.50 an hour at NZ$5 a hand; the swings are far larger than that average.

Frequently asked questions

Is live casino better than online casino games?

The odds are the same for the same rules. Live games are slower, so you place fewer bets per hour, which can mean a lower expected cost per hour. They also feel more transparent because you see the cards and wheel.

Are live dealer games rigged?

Major studios are audited and stream continuously. The bigger risk is the casino's own terms and payout record, which is what our reviews look at.

What is the best live casino game for odds?

Live blackjack with basic strategy, at about 0.5% house edge, then baccarat on the banker bet.

Can I play live casino on my phone?

Yes. Every casino here streams live tables in a mobile browser. Use Wi-Fi for a stable stream.

What are the minimum bets at live casinos?

Usually NZ$1 to NZ$5 on mainstream tables, set per table. VIP and high-limit tables start much higher.

Are live casino dealers real?

Yes. They work in studios run by providers such as Evolution and are filmed in real time.

Are live dealer games available 24/7 in NZ?

Most popular games run around the clock, but the number of open tables is lower in European night hours, which fall in the New Zealand afternoon.
