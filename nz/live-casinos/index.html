How live dealer casino games work

A live casino streams a real dealer, real cards and a real wheel from a studio, usually in Europe, Asia or Latin America. You place bets on an on-screen overlay during a betting window, then watch the result. Optical character recognition reads the cards and wheel results so the game can settle your bets automatically. Most casinos on this list carry tables from the large live studios, so the same games appear in many lobbies.

Live blackjack

Live blackjack uses the same rules as computer blackjack, so basic strategy keeps the house edge around 0.5%. Check two rules before you sit down: whether blackjack pays 3:2 (good) or 6:5 (much worse), and whether the dealer hits or stands on soft 17. Many live tables have a fixed number of seats; 'infinite' versions let any number of players play the same hand. Side bets such as Perfect Pairs and 21+3 carry much higher edges.

Live roulette

Choose European (single zero, 2.7% edge) or French (single zero, with La Partage on even-money bets, about 1.35%) over American (double zero, 5.26%). Multiplier variants such as Lightning Roulette add random multipliers on straight-up numbers but pay less on standard straight-up wins to fund them.

Live baccarat

Baccarat is a game of pure chance with three bets: banker (1.06% edge after commission), player (1.24%) and tie (about 14.4%). 'No commission' tables usually pay less on certain banker wins instead. Speed baccarat deals faster rounds, which means more bets per hour and more expected cost per hour.

Live game shows

Game shows such as money wheels with bonus rounds are entertainment-first. Their RTP varies by bet, often between about 95% and 97%, and the top multipliers are rare by design. They move fast, so set a time limit as well as a money limit.

Playing at New Zealand hours

Because most studios are in Europe, the busiest tables are often New Zealand early mornings, and New Zealand evenings line up with European mornings when fewer tables may be open. Studios in Asia are closer to New Zealand time. Stake ranges are set per table, commonly from NZ$1 to NZ$5 minimum on mainstream tables, with VIP tables much higher.

Etiquette, chat and connection

  • Use a stable connection; if you drop out mid-hand, the table's rules decide what happens to your bet.
  • Chat is moderated; dealers will not respond to abuse.
  • Take screenshots of any disputed result and note the round ID shown on screen.

Frequently asked questions

Is live casino better than online casino games?

The odds are the same for the same rules. Live games are slower, so you place fewer bets per hour, which can mean a lower expected cost per hour. They also feel more transparent because you see the cards and wheel.

Are live dealer games rigged?

Major studios are audited and stream continuously. The bigger risk is the casino's own terms and payout record, which is what our reviews look at.

What is the best live casino game for odds?

Live blackjack with basic strategy, at about 0.5% house edge, then baccarat on the banker bet.

Can I play live casino on my phone?

Yes. Every casino here streams live tables in a mobile browser. Use Wi-Fi for a stable stream.

What are the minimum bets at live casinos?

Usually NZ$1 to NZ$5 on mainstream tables, set per table. VIP and high-limit tables start much higher.
