How to sign up, claim a bonus and withdraw

The bonus decision happens at the deposit step. Make it deliberately, then follow the withdrawal steps once wagering is done.

Step 1: open your account

  1. Check the site before you sign up

    Find the licence number in the site's footer and look it up on the regulator's public register, then read our review for the owner, limits and complaint record. If the footer has no licence number, stop there.

  2. Register with your real details

    Use your legal name, date of birth, home address and an email you check, exactly as they appear on your ID. You must be 18 or over. Wrong or made-up details are the most common reason a withdrawal is refused later.

  3. Choose your currency carefully

    If NZD is offered, pick it at sign-up. Most sites will not let you change the account currency afterwards, and a non-NZD account means your bank converts every deposit and withdrawal.

  4. Set your limits first

    Before you deposit, open the account or responsible gambling settings and set a deposit limit, a loss limit and a session reminder. Lowering a limit takes effect straight away; raising one usually has a cooling-off delay.

  5. Verify your identity straight away

    Upload photo ID (passport or driver licence) and proof of address dated in the last three months, such as a bank statement or power bill. Doing it now means your first withdrawal is not held up waiting for checks.

Step 2: make a deposit

  1. Open the cashier

    Log in and choose Deposit or Cashier. Check the minimum deposit, usually around NZ$20 to NZ$30 or the euro equivalent, and whether the method you want carries a fee.

  2. Pick a method you can also withdraw to

    Cards, e-wallets such as Skrill, bank transfer and crypto all work both ways. Prepaid vouchers such as Neosurf are deposit-only, so you would need a second method to get money out.

  3. Decide on the bonus before you pay

    Tick or untick the bonus box, or leave the code field empty if you do not want it. Once a bonus is active, its wagering, maximum-bet and game rules apply to your balance until you clear or forfeit it.

  4. Enter the amount and confirm

    Stay inside the limit you set. Your bank may ask you to approve a card payment in its app. If a New Zealand bank declines the payment, it may block offshore gambling transactions; do not try to get around a block you set yourself.

  5. Check the balance and keep a record

    Card and e-wallet deposits usually show within a minute. Screenshot or note the transaction ID, the amount and the time, in case you need to raise a dispute.

Step 3: make a withdrawal

  1. Clear or cancel any bonus first

    Withdrawing while a bonus is active usually voids the bonus and anything won with it. Check your wagering progress in the account area, or forfeit the bonus if you would rather cash out what is yours.

  2. Request the withdrawal

    Open the cashier, choose Withdraw and enter the amount. Most sites send money back by the method you deposited with, so expect to use the same card, e-wallet or wallet address.

  3. Leave it pending

    Many sites hold withdrawals for 24 to 72 hours before processing them. Do not reverse the request to keep playing; turn off 'reverse withdrawal' if the site offers it.

  4. Answer verification requests quickly

    For larger amounts the site may ask for a recent bank statement or proof of where the money came from. Reply from the email on your account and keep copies of everything you send.

  5. Watch the limits

    Daily, weekly and monthly withdrawal limits mean a big win can be paid in instalments. The limit for each site is in its row above and in its review.

  6. Track the payment to your account

    Once approved, e-wallet and crypto withdrawals usually arrive the same day; cards and bank transfers take one to five working days. If nothing arrives, contact support with the transaction ID, then the regulator named on the licence.

How a casino bonus works

A deposit bonus adds money to your account that you cannot withdraw straight away. To convert it into cash, you must bet a set multiple of it, the wagering requirement (also called playthrough or rollover). Until then, the bonus and usually any winnings from it are locked. If you withdraw early, you normally lose both.

Wagering, worked out in dollars

The formula is simple: bonus × wagering multiple = amount you must bet. Some casinos apply the multiple to the deposit plus the bonus, which doubles it. Then amount bet × house edge = expected cost.

Read the last column carefully. At 40x on deposit plus bonus, a NZ$100 bonus has an average cost of NZ$320 to clear. That does not mean you will lose it, but on average the bonus costs more than it gives.

The terms that matter more than the headline

Types of casino bonus

  • Welcome package: matched deposits across your first few deposits, often with free spins.
  • Free spins: spins on a named pokie at a fixed stake; winnings usually carry wagering.
  • Reload: a smaller match on later deposits, often weekly.
  • Cashback: a percentage of net losses returned, sometimes as cash, sometimes as bonus money with wagering.
  • High-roller bonus: larger match for large deposits, for example CrownSlots' reported 100% up to €5,000 on €500 or more.
  • No deposit bonus: small amounts or spins without depositing; see no deposit bonuses.

When to say no to a bonus

Playing without a bonus is often the better choice. Decline it if you mainly play table or live games (they rarely count), if you want to withdraw quickly, if you like to bet more than the maximum stake, or if the expected cost in the table above is higher than the bonus itself.

What are casino bonus codes?

A code tells the cashier which offer to apply. Some casinos need a code for every stage of a welcome package; others apply the first stage automatically. A code found on a third-party site is no guarantee the offer exists: if the cashier rejects it, the published terms on the casino's own site are what count.

Which games are best for clearing a bonus?

Pokies usually count 100%; table and live games 0% to 20%. Within pokies, low-volatility games give steadier progress through wagering, while high-volatility games are more likely to bust the balance early or produce one large win. Many terms ban bonus buys and certain high-RTP games during wagering.

Payment methods that can void a bonus

Skrill and Neteller deposits are excluded from welcome offers at many casinos, and some exclude prepaid vouchers or crypto. Check the bonus terms for 'excluded payment methods' before you deposit.

High roller, live casino and crypto bonuses

High roller bonuses need a large first deposit for a higher cap. Live casino bonuses are rare because live games count little towards wagering; cashback is the usual alternative. Crypto bonuses may be quoted in coins, like Spino's reported 20% cashback up to 2,000 USDT.

Casino or sports welcome: choose one

Combined accounts usually let you take one welcome offer, not both. If you mainly bet on sport, the sports offer usually has the easier rollover.

Frequently asked questions

What is a good wagering requirement?

Under 30x on the bonus amount only is good for an offshore casino. Trixino's 7x on record is unusually low; 35x to 40x is normal.

What is the biggest casino bonus for NZ players?

By headline, Trixino's 777% up to €7,777 and Gunsbet's 285% up to €7,500 are the largest on our records. The size of the headline matters far less than the wagering.

Can I withdraw a casino bonus?

Only after you complete the wagering requirement within the time limit and follow the other terms. Withdrawing early usually cancels the bonus and its winnings.

Do table games count towards wagering?

Usually very little or not at all. Most casinos count pokies at 100% and table and live games at 0% to 10%.

Why was my bonus winnings cancelled?

Common reasons are breaking the maximum bet rule, playing excluded games, using a non-qualifying deposit method, or missing the time limit. Ask support for the exact term they relied on.

Should I take a welcome bonus?

Only if the expected cost of the wagering is lower than the bonus and the terms suit how you play. Otherwise, play with your own money and withdraw whenever you like.

Can I have more than one active bonus at a time?

Usually not. Most casinos queue a second bonus until the first is cleared or forfeited.

Can I use bonus buy on a pokie while wagering is active?

Usually not. Bonus buys are commonly banned during wagering and using one can void the bonus.

Are casino bonus winnings taxable in NZ?

Not for casual players. Inland Revenue does not treat gambling winnings as income.
