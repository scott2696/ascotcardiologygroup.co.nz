How to join a betting site, deposit and withdraw

Set up the account properly once and every later withdrawal is quicker. In New Zealand, only TAB NZ and Betcha may lawfully take your bets.

Step 1: open your betting account

  1. Check the site before you sign up

    Find the licence number in the site's footer and look it up on the regulator's public register, then read our review for the owner, limits and complaint record. If the footer has no licence number, stop there.

  2. Register with your real details

    Use your legal name, date of birth, home address and an email you check, exactly as they appear on your ID. You must be 18 or over. Wrong or made-up details are the most common reason a withdrawal is refused later.

  3. Choose your currency carefully

    If NZD is offered, pick it at sign-up. Most sites will not let you change the account currency afterwards, and a non-NZD account means your bank converts every deposit and withdrawal.

  4. Set your limits first

    Before you deposit, open the account or responsible gambling settings and set a deposit limit, a loss limit and a session reminder. Lowering a limit takes effect straight away; raising one usually has a cooling-off delay.

  5. Verify your identity straight away

    Upload photo ID (passport or driver licence) and proof of address dated in the last three months, such as a bank statement or power bill. Doing it now means your first withdrawal is not held up waiting for checks.

Step 2: make a deposit

  1. Open the cashier

    Log in and choose Deposit or Cashier. Check the minimum deposit, usually around NZ$20 to NZ$30 or the euro equivalent, and whether the method you want carries a fee.

  2. Pick a method you can also withdraw to

    Cards, e-wallets such as Skrill, bank transfer and crypto all work both ways. Prepaid vouchers such as Neosurf are deposit-only, so you would need a second method to get money out.

  3. Check the free bet or bonus terms

    Sports bonuses usually need the bonus to be rolled over several times at minimum odds, such as 1.80. Decide whether you want it before you deposit, and untick it if not.

  4. Enter the amount and confirm

    Stay inside the limit you set. Your bank may ask you to approve a card payment in its app. If a New Zealand bank declines the payment, it may block offshore gambling transactions; do not try to get around a block you set yourself.

  5. Check the balance and keep a record

    Card and e-wallet deposits usually show within a minute. Screenshot or note the transaction ID, the amount and the time, in case you need to raise a dispute.

Step 3: make a withdrawal

  1. Wait for open bets to settle

    Funds in unsettled bets cannot be withdrawn. Check the bet history and any rollover still needed on a bonus.

  2. Request the withdrawal

    Open the cashier, choose Withdraw and enter the amount. Most sites send money back by the method you deposited with, so expect to use the same card, e-wallet or wallet address.

  3. Leave it pending

    Many sites hold withdrawals for 24 to 72 hours before processing them. Do not reverse the request to keep playing; turn off 'reverse withdrawal' if the site offers it.

  4. Answer verification requests quickly

    For larger amounts the site may ask for a recent bank statement or proof of where the money came from. Reply from the email on your account and keep copies of everything you send.

  5. Watch the limits

    Daily, weekly and monthly withdrawal limits mean a big win can be paid in instalments. The limit for each site is in its row above and in its review.

  6. Track the payment to your account

    Once approved, e-wallet and crypto withdrawals usually arrive the same day; cards and bank transfers take one to five working days. If nothing arrives, contact support with the transaction ID, then the regulator named on the licence.

Betting law in New Zealand, in short

Since 28 June 2025, under the Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025, TAB NZ is the only operator allowed to offer online racing and sports betting to people in New Zealand, and offshore bookmakers may not take bets from New Zealanders. TAB NZ runs its betting through a 25-year partnership with Entain, which also operates the online brand Betcha alongside TAB. The offshore sportsbooks named on this site are covered for information only; we do not link to them or carry their offers.

What to compare between betting sites

Before the 2025 law, comparison sites ranked bookmakers on odds boosts and sign-up bets. For New Zealanders the first question is now legal: only TAB NZ may take your bets. For the offshore brands on this page, the facts that matter are the same as for casinos: who owns the site, which licence it holds, whether it pays in NZD, and how much it lets you withdraw.

Margins in practice

A two-way market priced 1.91 and 1.91 carries a margin of about 4.7%. Priced 1.80 and 1.80, it carries about 11.1%. Over a season of NZ$20 bets, that difference is the single biggest factor in what betting costs you. Our online betting guide shows how to work it out for any market.

Withdrawal limits and win caps

Several offshore books here cap what you can win or withdraw: Bet&Play has a daily win cap of US$50,000; Rivo and Smash cap daily wins at €100,000 and limit withdrawals to €1,000 a day. Combined casino and sportsbook accounts usually share one withdrawal limit across both.

Red flags at any betting site

  • Bets voided after a win for 'palpable error' without a clear explanation.
  • Stake limits imposed after a few wins, then withdrawals delayed.
  • Sign-up bets with rollover on odds of 1.80 or more, which forces many more bets.
  • No licence number, or an owner that is not named.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best sports betting site in NZ?

Legally, TAB NZ (including its partner brand Betcha) is the only online bookmaker allowed to take bets from people in New Zealand. The offshore sportsbooks on this page are listed for information only.

Why can't I bet with offshore bookmakers any more?

The Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025 made TAB NZ the only lawful provider of online racing and sports betting in New Zealand from 28 June 2025. Offshore operators are not permitted to take bets from New Zealanders.

Do offshore sportsbooks accept NZD?

Some list NZD as a balance currency, including Lucky Vibe, Rooster Bet, Fortune Play, Bet&Play and Gunsbet. That does not make them lawful providers in New Zealand.

Which betting site has the best odds?

It depends on the market and changes constantly. Compare margins: total the implied probabilities of every outcome; the lower the total above 100%, the better.

Are betting sites safe?

TAB NZ is regulated in New Zealand. Offshore sportsbooks are only as safe as their overseas licence and their own record, which is what our reviews check.

Is Betcha better than TAB?

They run on the same licence. Betcha is a newer online brand with a different app; TAB NZ has the deepest racing coverage. Compare prices on the events you bet on.

Can I get in trouble for using an overseas betting site?

The 2025 law targets the bookmakers and anyone promoting them, not individual punters, but offshore books may not take your bets and you have no New Zealand complaint route.
