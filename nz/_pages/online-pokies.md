## How online pokies work

Every online pokie is run by a random number generator (RNG): a program that picks a result for each spin the moment you press the button. The reels you see are an animation of a result that has already been decided. Spins are independent, so a machine is never 'due' a win and a run of losses does not make the next spin any more likely to pay. Games from the major studios are tested by independent labs that check the RNG and the published maths.

What you can control is which game you play, at what stake, for how long, and at which RTP setting. Those choices change your expected cost far more than any strategy on the reels.

## RTP, and why the same pokie can pay differently

Return to player (RTP) is the share of all money staked on a game that it pays back over a very large number of spins. A 96% RTP pokie returns NZ$96 for every NZ$100 staked on average, so the expected cost is about 4 cents per dollar wagered. Over one session, results swing wildly; RTP only describes the long run.

The detail most guides leave out: many popular pokies are sold to casinos at several RTP settings, for example 96.5%, 94% and 91%. The game looks identical. The casino chooses the version. On NZ$2,000 of spins, the difference between 96% and 91% is about NZ$100 in expected losses.Averages over the long run. Any single session can be far better or worse.

| RTP setting | Expected cost per NZ$100 wagered | On NZ$2,000 of spins |
|---|---|---|

| 91% | NZ$9 | NZ$180 |

How to check the RTP a casino is running

Open the game's information or paytable screen (often an 'i' or menu icon) and look for 'RTP' or 'theoretical return'. If the figure shown is lower than the one on the studio's own website, the casino is running a lower setting. 

## Volatility: how a pokie spends your money

Volatility (or variance) describes how results are spread. Low-volatility pokies pay small amounts often, so a budget lasts longer. High-volatility pokies pay rarely but can pay big, so long losing runs are normal. Two games with the same 96% RTP can feel completely different: one drip-feeds wins, the other can take your whole session budget before a bonus round lands. Choose volatility to match your budget, not your hopes.

## Progressive jackpots

A progressive jackpot grows with every bet placed on the game across all the casinos linked to it, until one player wins it. The headline can run into millions, but the chance of hitting it on any spin is tiny and the base game usually has a lower RTP to pay for the pot. If you play jackpot pokies, check the casino's withdrawal limits first: several sites on this list cap withdrawals at a few thousand dollars a week, and some exempt progressive jackpots from those limits while others do not.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which online pokies pay out the most in NZ?

Look for games with an RTP of 96% or more, and check the setting in the game's information panel, because the same title can run at a lower RTP at some casinos. No pokie 'pays more' in the short term; RTP is a long-run average. 

### Can you win real money on online pokies?

Yes, but on average every pokie returns less than you stake. Wins are random, and over time the house edge means most players lose more than they win. Play with money you are happy to spend on entertainment. 

### Are online pokies rigged?

Games from the major studios are tested by independent labs and use certified random number generators. The real risk is less the game than the casino: which RTP version it runs and whether it pays out. That is what our reviews check. 

### What is the difference between RTP and volatility?

RTP tells you how much a pokie pays back on average over the long run. Volatility tells you how unevenly it pays: often and small, or rarely and big. 

### Do I pay tax on pokie winnings in New Zealand?

Not if you play for fun. Casual gambling winnings are not treated as income by Inland Revenue. See [gambling winnings tax](https://ascotcardiologygroup.co.nz/nz/gambling-winnings-tax-nz/). 

### Are free pokies the same as real-money pokies?

The game and features are the same, but the demo may run at a different RTP setting from the real-money version a casino offers, and demo wins cannot be withdrawn.
