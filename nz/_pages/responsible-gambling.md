## Warning signs

- Spending more time or money than you meant to, more often.
- Chasing losses, or gambling to win back money you need.
- Borrowing, using credit, or selling things to gamble.
- Hiding gambling from your partner or whānau, or lying about it.
- Feeling restless, anxious or low when you are not gambling.
- Gambling to escape stress, loneliness or problems.

Two or more of these is a reason to pause and talk to someone. The Gambling Helpline is a good first call.

## Tools that work

| Tool | How it helps | Where to find it |
|---|---|---|
| Deposit limit | Caps what you can deposit per day, week or month | Account settings at most casinos and betting sites |
| Loss and session limits | Stops play after a set loss or time | Account settings, where offered |
| Reality checks | Pop-ups showing how long you have played | Account settings |
| Time-out | Locks your account for a short period | Account settings or support |
| Self-exclusion | Closes your account for months or years | Support or account settings |
| Bank gambling block | Blocks gambling payments on your cards | Your bank's app or phone line |

## Bank gambling blocks

Most major New Zealand banks offer a way to block gambling transactions on your cards, usually with a delay before the block can be lifted. It is one of the strongest tools available because it applies to every gambling site at once, offshore or local. Look in your banking app's card settings, or ask your bank.

## Self-exclusion

Each casino or betting site handles its own self-exclusion, so excluding from one does not exclude you from others, including sister sites from the same group. If you exclude, do it with every site you use, and combine it with a bank block. For land-based venues in New Zealand, venue exclusion orders are available, and the Gambling Helpline can help you arrange multi-venue exclusion.

## Blocking software

Gambling-blocking apps and browser tools can stop gambling sites loading on your devices. Some are free. Set them up on every device you use and, if possible, ask someone you trust to hold the password.

## If someone you care about is gambling too much

Talk when you are both calm, focus on how it is affecting you rather than on blame, and do not lend money to cover losses. Support services help family and whānau as well as gamblers: you can call the Gambling Helpline or the Problem Gambling Foundation for yourself.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I know if I have a gambling problem?

If gambling is costing more money or time than you can afford, if you chase losses, borrow to gamble or hide it, it is worth talking to someone. The Gambling Helpline on 0800 654 655 is free and confidential. 

### Can I block gambling on my bank card in New Zealand?

Yes, most major New Zealand banks let you block gambling transactions on your cards, usually in the banking app. 

### How do I self-exclude from online casinos?

Contact each casino's support or use the self-exclusion option in your account. Exclusion at one site does not cover others, so repeat it everywhere you have an account and add a bank block. 

### Is help free in New Zealand?

Yes. The Gambling Helpline, the Problem Gambling Foundation and Asian Family Services provide free, confidential support.
