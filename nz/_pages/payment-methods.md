Choosing how to pay is about more than speed. It decides what conversion fees you pay, how easily you can stop, and how quickly money comes back. Here is every common method from a New Zealand point of view.

## Visa and Mastercard debit and credit cards

Cards are accepted almost everywhere and deposits are instant. Two things trip New Zealanders up. First, some banks decline card payments to offshore gambling merchants, and if you have switched on a gambling block they will always be declined. Second, if the casino holds your balance in euros or US dollars, your bank applies a foreign currency fee and its own exchange rate on every transaction, commonly 2% to 3% combined. Not every card can receive withdrawals; casinos often pay card deposits back by bank transfer instead. Using a credit card to gamble means paying interest on losses, which is a good reason not to.

## E-wallets: Skrill, Neteller, MiFinity, Jeton, MuchBetter

E-wallets sit between your bank and the casino. Deposits are instant and withdrawals are usually the quickest fiat option once approved. They also keep gambling money in a separate place, which helps with budgeting. Watch for two catches: some casinos exclude Skrill and Neteller deposits from bonuses, and the e-wallet itself may charge for currency conversion and for withdrawing to your New Zealand bank.

## Prepaid vouchers: Neosurf, Paysafecard, Flexepin, CashtoCode

Vouchers are bought for cash or by card from a retailer or online, then entered as a code at the casino. They cap what you can spend and keep your bank details away from the casino. They are deposit only, so you will need a bank transfer, e-wallet or crypto to withdraw.

## Bank transfer

Direct bank transfers work at many casinos but are the slowest route, commonly two to seven business days, and international transfers can carry fees at both ends. New Zealand banks may ask questions about transfers to offshore gambling companies, and if you have a gambling block in place they may stop them.

## Cryptocurrency: Bitcoin, Ethereum, USDT, Litecoin and others

Most casinos on our list accept several coins. Crypto is often the most direct withdrawal route offshore, but it brings its own costs and risks: exchange fees in New Zealand, network fees, price swings while you hold the coins, irreversible transfers, and possible tax on crypto gains. ## POLi and Apple Pay

POLi, the bank-to-bank payment service, closed in Australia in 2023 but still operates in New Zealand. Apple Pay appears at a few brands on our list, including MadCasino, Smash and Rivo, usually as a way of paying by card.

Most major New Zealand banks let customers block gambling transactions on their cards, often with a switch in the banking app and a cooling-off period before it can be turned off. If you want a firm limit, this is one of the most effective tools there is, because it works at every gambling site at once. Check your bank's app, or call them. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best payment method for online casinos in NZ?

It depends on what you value. E-wallets balance speed and separation from your main account; vouchers cap your spending; crypto is often the most direct withdrawal route but carries price and tax risk. Paying into a casino that holds NZD avoids conversion fees whichever you choose. 

### Why was my card declined at an online casino?

Many New Zealand banks decline payments to offshore gambling merchants, and a gambling block will always decline them. Your card provider may also flag the payment for fraud checks. 

### Is POLi still available in New Zealand?

Yes, POLi still operates in New Zealand; it closed in Australia in 2023. It is rarely offered by offshore casinos. 

### Can I use PayPal at online casinos in NZ?

None of the payment lists we checked for the brands on this site includes PayPal. 

### Are casino deposits charged fees?

Casinos rarely charge deposit fees themselves, but your bank or e-wallet may charge for currency conversion, and crypto carries network and exchange fees.
