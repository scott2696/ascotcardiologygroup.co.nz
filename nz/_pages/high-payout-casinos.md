## What "best paying casino" actually means

Casinos do not set one payout rate for the whole site. Every game has its own return to player, so a site's 'payout percentage' is just an average of whatever its customers happened to play. Two things genuinely make one casino pay better than another: the **RTP versions** it chooses to run, and the **limits** it puts on how much you can withdraw. A casino running high-RTP versions but capping withdrawals at NZ$1,000 a day is not a high payout casino for anyone who wins big.

## The house edge on every main game
Standard published game maths. Live dealer versions use the same rules as their RNG equivalents.

| Game | RTP | House edge | What changes it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blackjack, basic strategy | about 99.5% | about 0.5% | Rules (dealer stands on soft 17, 3:2 payouts) and your decisions |
| Baccarat, banker bet | 98.94% | 1.06% | 5% commission on banker wins |
| Baccarat, player bet | 98.76% | 1.24% | Nothing |
| European roulette | 97.30% | 2.70% | Single zero; French 'La Partage' rules halve it on even-money bets |
| American roulette | 94.74% | 5.26% | Double zero; avoid |
| Video poker (full pay) | 99%+ | under 1% | Paytable and perfect strategy |
| Pokies | 88% to 97% | 3% to 12% | The RTP version the casino runs |
| Baccarat, tie bet | about 85.6% | about 14.4% | Avoid |

Expected cost is house edge times the amount you wager. NZ$1,000 wagered on European roulette costs about NZ$27 on average; the same NZ$1,000 on a 94% pokie costs about NZ$60, and on basic-strategy blackjack about NZ$5.

## Why RTP settings matter more than the casino

Studios commonly offer the same pokie at several RTP levels. The casino picks one. You can check which is running by opening the game's paytable or information screen and comparing the RTP with the figure on the studio's website. If a casino consistently runs the lower settings, its pokies are a worse deal everywhere in the lobby, however generous its bonus looks.

## How to get the best return

- Choose games with a low house edge: blackjack with basic strategy, baccarat banker, European roulette.
- On pokies, check the RTP setting and prefer 96% or more.
- Skip side bets and tie bets, which carry far higher edges.
- Avoid bonuses whose wagering would force you to bet many times your deposit.
- Pick a casino whose withdrawal limits match the size of win you could realistically land.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which online casino has the highest payout in NZ?

No casino has a single payout rate. What you can compare is the RTP of the games and the withdrawal limits. The best-documented NZD sites publish the highest limits on our list, at NZ$3,000 a day and NZ$7,500 a week. 

### What casino game has the best odds?

Blackjack played with basic strategy, at about 0.5% house edge, followed by baccarat on the banker bet (1.06%) and European roulette (2.7%). 

### Is a 98% RTP pokie better than a 96% one?

Over the long run, yes: it costs about 2 cents per dollar wagered instead of 4. In a single session, volatility matters far more than that difference. 

### Why do some casinos have different RTP for the same game?

Studios sell many games at several RTP settings and the casino chooses which one to run. The game looks the same; only the paytable information shows the difference. 

### Do withdrawal limits affect payouts?

Yes. A big win is paid in instalments up to the limit, so a low weekly limit can turn a large win into months of payments. A daily win cap means winnings above it are not paid at all.
