## How to open a betting account, deposit and withdraw

The steps are the same at any bookmaker. In New Zealand, only TAB NZ and Betcha may lawfully take your bets.

### Step 1: open your betting account

1. 

#### Check the site before you sign up

Find the licence number in the site's footer and look it up on the regulator's public register, then read our review for the owner, limits and complaint record. If the footer has no licence number, stop there.
2. 

#### Register with your real details

Use your legal name, date of birth, home address and an email you check, exactly as they appear on your ID. You must be 18 or over. Wrong or made-up details are the most common reason a withdrawal is refused later.
3. 

#### Choose your currency carefully

If NZD is offered, pick it at sign-up. Most sites will not let you change the account currency afterwards, and a non-NZD account means your bank converts every deposit and withdrawal.
4. 

#### Set your limits first

Before you deposit, open the account or responsible gambling settings and set a deposit limit, a loss limit and a session reminder. Lowering a limit takes effect straight away; raising one usually has a cooling-off delay.
5. 

#### Verify your identity straight away

Upload photo ID (passport or driver licence) and proof of address dated in the last three months, such as a bank statement or power bill. Doing it now means your first withdrawal is not held up waiting for checks.

### Step 2: make a deposit

1. 

#### Open the cashier

Log in and choose Deposit or Cashier. Check the minimum deposit, usually around NZ$20 to NZ$30 or the euro equivalent, and whether the method you want carries a fee.
2. 

#### Pick a method you can also withdraw to

Cards, e-wallets such as Skrill, bank transfer and crypto all work both ways. Prepaid vouchers such as Neosurf are deposit-only, so you would need a second method to get money out.
3. 

#### Check the free bet or bonus terms

Sports bonuses usually need the bonus to be rolled over several times at minimum odds, such as 1.80. Decide whether you want it before you deposit, and untick it if not.
4. 

#### Enter the amount and confirm

Stay inside the limit you set. Your bank may ask you to approve a card payment in its app. If a New Zealand bank declines the payment, it may block offshore gambling transactions; do not try to get around a block you set yourself.
5. 

#### Check the balance and keep a record

Card and e-wallet deposits usually show within a minute. Screenshot or note the transaction ID, the amount and the time, in case you need to raise a dispute.

### Step 3: make a withdrawal

1. 

#### Wait for open bets to settle

Funds in unsettled bets cannot be withdrawn. Check the bet history and any rollover still needed on a bonus.
2. 

#### Request the withdrawal

Open the cashier, choose Withdraw and enter the amount. Most sites send money back by the method you deposited with, so expect to use the same card, e-wallet or wallet address.
3. 

#### Leave it pending

Many sites hold withdrawals for 24 to 72 hours before processing them. Do not reverse the request to keep playing; turn off 'reverse withdrawal' if the site offers it.
4. 

#### Answer verification requests quickly

For larger amounts the site may ask for a recent bank statement or proof of where the money came from. Reply from the email on your account and keep copies of everything you send.
5. 

#### Watch the limits

Daily, weekly and monthly withdrawal limits mean a big win can be paid in instalments. The limit for each site is in its row above and in its review.
6. 

#### Track the payment to your account

Once approved, e-wallet and crypto withdrawals usually arrive the same day; cards and bank transfers take one to five working days. If nothing arrives, contact support with the transaction ID, then the regulator named on the licence.

## Online betting law in New Zealand since June 2025

The Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025 passed on 27 June 2025 and took effect the next day. It extended TAB NZ's long-standing exclusive right to offer racing and sports betting so that it clearly covers online betting, and it closed the door on offshore bookmakers that had been taking bets from New Zealanders without paying into New Zealand racing or sport. Offshore operators are not allowed to offer betting to people in New Zealand.

For punters, the practical effect is that a legal online bet in New Zealand is a bet with TAB NZ or its partner brand. Offshore sportsbooks may still be visible online, but they are not authorised here, they cannot lawfully advertise to you, and you have no New Zealand regulator to turn to if one refuses to pay.

## TAB NZ and Betcha

TAB NZ is a statutory body set up under the Racing Industry Act 2020. In 2023 it agreed a 25-year partnership with Entain, a large international betting company, which now runs TAB NZ's betting products. Entain also launched Betcha, an online betting brand that operates alongside TAB NZ under the same legal framework. Both offer fixed-odds sports betting; TAB NZ also runs pari-mutuel (tote) racing pools.

A monopoly has trade-offs. Supporters point to funding for racing and sport and to New Zealand harm-reduction rules. Critics, including some punters, say odds and limits are less competitive than offshore books used to be. Either way, it is the legal position. 

## How betting odds work

New Zealand bookmakers use decimal odds. The number is what a winning NZ$1 bet returns, including the stake. Odds of 3.00 return NZ$3 for every NZ$1, a profit of NZ$2. The odds also tell you the probability the bookmaker is pricing in: divide 1 by the odds.Implied probability = 1 ÷ decimal odds. Returns include your stake.

| Decimal odds | Fractional | Implied probability | NZ$10 bet returns |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.50 | 1/2 | 66.7% | NZ$15.00 |
| 1.91 | 10/11 | 52.4% | NZ$19.10 |
| 2.00 | evens | 50.0% | NZ$20.00 |
| 3.00 | 2/1 | 33.3% | NZ$30.00 |
| 5.00 | 4/1 | 20.0% | NZ$50.00 |
| 11.00 | 10/1 | 9.1% | NZ$110.00 |

## Bookmaker margins: the number that decides value

Add up the implied probabilities of every outcome in a market. In a fair market they would total 100%. They always total more, and the excess is the bookmaker's margin (also called the overround or vig). It is the built-in cost of betting, in the same way the house edge is the cost of casino games.The lower the margin, the better the value for the same outcome.

| Market | Implied probabilities | Total | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| Two-way market, 1.91 / 1.91 | 52.36% + 52.36% | 104.7% | 4.7% |
| Two-way market, 1.95 / 1.87 | 51.28% + 53.48% | 104.8% | 4.8% |
| Two-way market, 1.80 / 1.80 | 55.56% + 55.56% | 111.1% | 11.1% |
| Three-way football, 2.40 / 3.30 / 3.00 | 41.7% + 30.3% + 33.3% | 105.3% | 5.3% |

Margins are usually lowest on the main markets of big events, such as a head-to-head on an All Blacks test, and highest on niche markets, same-game multis and long-range futures.

## Live (in-play) betting and cash-out

Live betting lets you bet after a match starts, with odds updating every few seconds. It is the fastest form of betting and the easiest way to bet more than you planned. Cash-out lets you settle a bet early for less than its full return, or to cut a loss. The cash-out price includes a margin of its own, so cashing out regularly costs money over time.

## Betting and tax

Inland Revenue generally does not tax casual gambling winnings, including betting wins, because they are not income from a business. If you bet with the regularity, organisation and intention of a business, winnings can be taxable and losses may be deductible. See [gambling winnings tax](https://ascotcardiologygroup.co.nz/nz/gambling-winnings-tax-nz/).

## Betting safely

- Set a deposit limit in your account before your first bet.

- Never chase losses with bigger bets or extra multis.

## Why live bets are delayed and markets suspend

In-play bets carry a delay of a few seconds so the bookmaker can check that the price has not moved. Markets suspend whenever something significant may happen, such as a try, a wicket or a penalty. If your bet is rejected, the price changed during the delay.

## Rugby, league and cricket in-play

Rugby markets move most after tries and cards; line (handicap) betting is the main market. In league, momentum swings after sin-bins. In cricket, prices move ball by ball, so in-play delays are longest and suspensions most frequent.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is online betting legal in New Zealand?

Yes, with TAB NZ. Since 28 June 2025, TAB NZ has been the only operator allowed to offer online racing and sports betting to people in New Zealand. Offshore bookmakers are not permitted to take bets from New Zealanders. 

### Can I still use offshore betting sites from New Zealand?

The 2025 law is aimed at the bookmakers: offshore operators may not offer betting to New Zealanders, and many now block New Zealand accounts. If you hold an offshore account, withdraw your balance, and be aware you have no New Zealand protection if a dispute arises. 

### What is Betcha?

Betcha is an online betting brand run by Entain alongside TAB NZ, under the 25-year partnership between the two that was agreed in 2023. It operates within TAB NZ's legal framework. 

### What are the best odds for betting in NZ?

Odds vary by market and change constantly. The way to judge value is the bookmaker's margin: add up the implied probabilities of every outcome. The closer to 100%, the better the value. 

### How do I work out implied probability from odds?

Divide 1 by the decimal odds. Odds of 2.50 imply 1 ÷ 2.50 = 40%. If you think the real chance is higher than 40%, the bet is good value; if lower, it is not. 

### Are betting winnings taxed in New Zealand?

Not for recreational punters. Inland Revenue generally treats casual gambling winnings, including betting, as non-taxable. Professional gambling can be different. 

### What is a multi, and why do they pay so much?

A multi combines several selections, and every one must win. The odds multiply, and so does the bookmaker's margin, so long multis carry a much larger built-in edge than single bets. 

### What happened to greyhound racing betting?

Commercial greyhound racing in New Zealand ended on 1 August 2026 under the Racing Industry (Closure of Greyhound Racing Industry) Amendment Act, passed in April 2026. 

### How can I limit my betting?

Set deposit limits in your betting account, use your bank's gambling block, and call the Gambling Helpline on [0800 654 655](tel:0800654655) if betting is causing harm. 

### Why are live bets delayed?

A delay of a few seconds lets the bookmaker confirm the price has not changed. If it has, the bet is rejected or re-priced. 

### Is cash out guaranteed on live bets?

No. Cash out can be suspended at any time, especially during key moments of a match.
