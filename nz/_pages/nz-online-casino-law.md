## The short answer

It is not an offence for a New Zealander to play at an online casino based overseas. It is unlawful for an unlicensed online casino, or anyone acting for it, to advertise to people in New Zealand, and from 1 December 2026 unlicensed casinos that have not applied for a New Zealand licence must stop serving New Zealand. Eleven companies won the right to apply in the DIA's October 2026 auction, and the first New Zealand licences are expected in 2027.

## Before 2026: the Gambling Act 2003

The Gambling Act 2003 banned 'remote interactive gambling' from being run inside New Zealand, with exceptions for the TAB and the Lotteries Commission (Lotto NZ). It did not stop New Zealanders using casinos based overseas, and it already prohibited advertising overseas gambling here. In practice that advertising ban was hard to enforce, and offshore casinos built a large New Zealand customer base.

## The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026

The Online Casino Gambling Act 2026 received royal assent and came into force on 1 May 2026. It creates a licensed online casino market regulated by the Department of Internal Affairs. Its main features:

- Up to **15 licences** , allocated by auction, each for one brand and valid for up to three years.
- No company may hold more than three licences.
- Licensed operators must take reasonable steps to verify that players are at least **18** .
- Regulations covering harm prevention and minimisation, consumer protection and record-keeping, advertising and marketing, and fees and levies.
- A public register of licensed operators maintained by the DIA.

## The advertising ban

Advertising online casino gambling to people in New Zealand has been prohibited for years, but the 2026 Act gave the DIA new enforcement powers from 1 May 2026: takedown notices and pecuniary penalties of up to **NZ$5 million** for unlawful advertising. According to the DIA, these can be issued to anyone who publishes an unlawful advertisement or arranges to publish one on behalf of an online casino provider, which includes influencers, content creators and affiliate sites.

After licensing, licensed operators will be allowed to advertise with restrictions. Cabinet's initial regulatory decisions, released in late 2025, include prohibiting affiliate marketing and paid endorsements. This is why Kiwi Casino Ledger carries no links to casinos and no sign-up offers.

Reporting advertising

You can report unlawful online casino advertising to the DIA at onlinegambling@dia.govt.nz.

## What licensed casinos will have to do

Licensed operators will be held to the same standards, set in regulations covering harm minimisation, consumer protection and advertising. Expect identity and age checks, harm-prevention tools, rules on inducements, and a complaints route through the operator and then the DIA. Applicants must submit detailed plans for advertising and marketing, consumer protection, harm prevention and compliance before a licence is granted.

## Sports and racing betting

Betting is covered by different law. The Racing Industry Amendment Act 2025, in force from 28 June 2025, made TAB NZ the only operator allowed to offer online racing and sports betting to people in New Zealand. Offshore bookmakers may not take bets from New Zealanders. See [online betting NZ](https://ascotcardiologygroup.co.nz/nz/online-betting/).

## Lotto and land-based gambling

Lotto NZ remains the lawful operator of national lotteries, including online through MyLotto. Land-based casinos operate under the Gambling Act 2003, with their own licensing and venue rules, and class 4 gaming machines (pokies in pubs and clubs) are run by licensed societies that return funds to the community.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is online gambling legal in New Zealand?

Playing at an offshore online casino is not an offence for individuals. Running online casino gambling in New Zealand without a licence, and advertising unlicensed online casinos here, are unlawful. Online betting is lawful only with TAB NZ, and lotteries with Lotto NZ. 

### When will licensed online casinos be available in New Zealand?

The auction closed on 12 October 2026 and full applications followed. The DIA expects the licensed market to be fully operational during 2027, and the exemption for pending applicants ends on 1 June 2027. 

### How many online casino licences will New Zealand issue?

Up to 15, each for a single brand and valid for up to three years. No company may hold more than three. 

### What is the minimum age for online casinos in New Zealand?

Licensed online casinos must take reasonable steps to verify that players are at least 18. 

### Can I be fined for playing at an offshore casino?

The penalties in the 2026 Act are aimed at operators and advertisers, not at individual players. 

### What happens to offshore casinos after 1 December 2026?

Those that have not applied for a licence must stop offering online casino gambling in New Zealand. Applicants may continue without advertising until their application is decided. Only the 11 companies that won at auction could apply. 

### Who won New Zealand's online casino licence auction?

Eleven companies won the right to apply: Baytree Interactive (3 rights), Playvia Gaming (NZ) (2), Rock Swift Group (2), Christchurch Online NZ, One Casino, Rush Entertainment, Skill On Net, SkyCity NZ Online NO1 and NO2, Stake New Zealand and TAB New Zealand. The price reached NZ$15 million per licence. 

### Will affiliate casino sites be allowed?

Cabinet's initial regulatory decisions include prohibiting affiliate marketing and paid endorsements, even for licensed operators.
