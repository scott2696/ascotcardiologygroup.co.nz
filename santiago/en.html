02 — The practice

Who translates, on what qualification, and under what standards the work is issued.

Qualified translators, accountable for every page they sign.

No file is farmed out to a marketplace of anonymous translators. Your documents are assigned to one translator of the practice, whose name and qualifications appear on the certificate you submit.

The practice works with a deliberately small number of translators, each holding a postgraduate qualification in translation and demonstrable experience in legal and institutional texts between Spanish and English. That grounding is what allows a translation to survive the scrutiny it will actually face: a case officer comparing names and dates against the original, a registrar checking that a seal has been accounted for, counsel confirming that a clause still means what it meant.

Certified translation is a documentary discipline rather than a literary one. Stamps, marginal annotations, apostilles, abbreviations, official titles and grading scales all carry evidentiary weight, and each must be rendered faithfully and positioned so the reviewing officer can follow the original line by line.

Every file is issued with a signed and dated certificate of accuracy stating the qualifications of the translator responsible and confirming the translation is complete and faithful to the source document — the form institutions expect, and the reason so few of our files come back with queries.

07 — Questions

Answered before you send anything.

Matters raised most often.

Will the translation be accepted by the receiving institution?

Every translation is issued with a signed and dated certificate of accuracy stating my name, qualifications and confirmation that the translation is a complete and faithful rendering of the original — the form required by immigration authorities, universities, courts and consulates. Where an institution publishes its own requirements, send them with your documents and the file will be prepared to match.

How long does a certified translation take?

Standard single-page civil documents are delivered within 24 to 48 hours of instruction. Longer academic and legal files depend on length and formatting, and a delivery date is committed in the quotation. Same-day handling is available where a deadline requires it.

Must I post the original document?

In almost all cases, no. A clear scan or a well-lit photograph is sufficient, provided every word, seal and stamp is legible. Where an institution requires sight of a physical original or a notarised hard copy, say so at quotation stage and it will be arranged.

How is the fee determined?

By word count, complexity, formatting, legibility of the source, deadline, and any additional requirement such as notarisation, apostille handling or dispatch of hard copies. The fee is fixed in writing once the document has been seen, and does not change afterwards.

How is confidentiality handled?

Documents are handled by me alone, never passed to third parties or uploaded to public tools, and permanently deleted from my systems on request once the file has been delivered. A signed non-disclosure agreement is available to corporate and legal clients.

Can notarisation or an apostille be arranged?

In many jurisdictions, yes — notarisation of the certification can be arranged, and I can advise on the apostille process for the destination country. Raise it when requesting your quotation so that cost and timing are accounted for from the outset.
